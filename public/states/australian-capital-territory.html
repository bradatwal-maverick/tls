The Australian Capital Territory rules

  • Builder licences come in four classes: A (any height), B (up to 3 storeys), C (homes up to 2 storeys) and D (non-structural basic work).
  • Classes A to C need a building qualification (a Cert IV for Class C, a Diploma for Class B, a degree for Class A) and 3,650 hours of experience, with 1,825 of those after your qualification.
  • Class D needs 5,475 hours of experience and no qualification.
  • Classes A to C also need a pass in the ACT Regulatory Assessment at CIT. It takes 30 to 60 minutes and the pass mark is 80%.
  • A company or partnership needs its own licence, with at least one nominee who holds the same class of licence.

Official regulator: Access Canberra. Rules change, so check the regulator page too.

Your steps to a Australian Capital Territory licence

  1. Work out which class fits your work: A, B, C or D.
  2. Book and sit the ACT Regulatory Assessment at CIT (Classes A to C).
  3. Gather your qualification, proof of your hours on site and referees.
  4. Apply to Access Canberra, with us checking every document first.

Australian Capital Territory licence questions

Who licenses builders in the ACT?

Access Canberra assesses applications and issues construction licences for the Construction Occupations Registrar.

Are there exams for ACT builder licences?

Yes, for Classes A, B and C. You sit the ACT Regulatory Assessment at CIT in person. It takes 30 to 60 minutes and the pass mark is 80%. You need it for your first licence, or if your licence expired more than 12 months ago.

Which ACT builder class do I need?

It depends on the building. Class C covers homes up to 2 storeys, Class B up to 3 storeys and Class A any height. Class D covers non-structural basic work.

How much does an ACT licence cost?

Can I get an ACT licence from NSW?

Usually, yes. Mutual recognition can let a current licence from another state or New Zealand get you the equivalent licence in the ACT, as long as it covers the same work. Dan checks the match before you apply.

Licensed interstate?

Licensed in another state or New Zealand? Mutual recognition can let you get the equivalent licence in the ACT, if your licence is current and covers the same work. Dan checks the match before you apply. Official information.
